# dirt_shift – Functional specification
*v1.0*

## Purpose

`dirt_shift` shifts battery discharge deliberately into the grid hours with the highest CO₂ intensity. The German electricity mix is less favourable in the evening and at night (PV gone, evening load high, fossil peaking) and in the early morning until enough PV is on the grid; its CO₂ intensity is low around midday. Where one can choose *when* the battery covers load instead of the grid, doing so during the highest-intensity hours avoids the most emissions. `dirt_shift` therefore directs the entire available battery content into those hours — most strongly into the dirtiest one.

CO₂ intensity is a pure **grid** property and does not depend on the installation. The installation (PV, consumption, battery content) only determines the *amount* of energy available and needed.

`dirt_shift` steers battery discharge based on grid CO₂ intensity. It writes the same `timer.txt` that zeroinput reads for discharge control.

Direct PV pass-through (`pvpt`) is guaranteed, except for the optional precharge path (`precharge_enabled`, see there) — there, and only there, it can be throttled in the very cleanest hours to deliberately divert PV surplus into the battery instead of the house. Outside that path (the default), it means: power currently generated by PV is always passed straight through to cover house consumption, without the detour through the battery. That has the best efficiency (no charge/discharge loss) and spares the battery (no extra cycle). Outside the precharge path, `dirt_shift` acts exclusively on **battery discharge**, never on `pvpt`.

## Data sources

All data comes from volkszähler:

- **basic_load** — actual house consumption in Wh/h, computed in the default formula as `Import + |Inverter| − Auto`. A 7-day hourly average, cached hourly in `dirt_avg_cache.json`. Used to size the red reserve and the cap outside the dirtiest hour (see Red reserve and the dirtiest hour).
- **Energy content** — the real battery content in Wh, reconstructed via `get_vz_bat_cap` by integrating PV and inverter power since the last known 'empty' state (voltage ≤ 3.0625 V/cell as the anchor, i.e. 49 V at 16 cells; scaled by `cell_count` from the zeroinput config), with efficiencies applied. Only a **sustained** empty state anchors: the voltage must stay below the threshold for at least `EMPTY_MIN_DURATION_S` (code constant, 120 s). A brief dip — a load spike, an inverter start, an outage of a few seconds — would otherwise anchor mid-charge and read content too low from then on. In addition, voltage samples **below 2.5 V/cell** (`IMPLAUSIBLE_V_PER_CELL`, 40 V at 16S — the LiFePO₄ discharge cut-off below which every BMS disconnects) are discarded on read: they are physically impossible and therefore a sensor or connection fault. This also catches a *sustained* outage that the duration test alone would let through. Freshly queried on every run.

**Warning on negative content.** A reconstructed content below −`NEG_CONTENT_WARN_FRACTION` (code constant, 5%) of `battery_capacity_wh` is physically impossible — more discharge than charge has been counted since the last empty state — and is always reported, even without `-v`. `_neg_content_diagnosis` tells two causes apart using the same channel balance with **no** efficiency applied at all (`raw_cap`): since both efficiencies can only push content down, never up, `raw_cap` is a solid upper bound. If `raw_cap` also breaches the limit, no efficiency correction could explain that away — the channel data itself is wrong, typically an inverter that *requests* power but does not *deliver* it (a tripped RCD, a blown fuse, a failed unit). If `raw_cap` stays within the limit, `PV_to_bat_efficiency`/`bat_to_AC_efficiency` may simply be set lower than the installation's real losses — the message still names both possibilities in this case, since a smaller channel fault cannot be ruled out.
- **PV generation** — the same PV channel is also used for the empirical PV reference curve (see there); raw values are treated with `abs()` there, since this channel is logged negative on many installations.

> **basic_load is freely adaptable.** The formula `Import + |Inverter| − Auto` reflects one particular installation (with an EV wallbox as a separate channel, subtracted from house consumption). It is **not a standard** — it must be adapted to your own installation: absent channels are dropped, additional ones are added. Without a separately metered wallbox, `Auto` disappears; a further separately metered load (e.g. a PV-battery charger) would come in as an additional subtraction term. Only **schedulable** loads that should not be covered from the reserve are subtracted (the car is charged deliberately, see Discharge by zone). Demand-driven loads such as air conditioning stay **in** basic_load — they belong to the consumption that must be covered and are captured, within limits, by the 7-day average. What matters is that basic_load ends up as the **actual house consumption to be covered** — i.e. grid import plus the inverter power delivered from the battery, net of anything that should not be covered from battery/grid. The calculation lives in `get_average` and is edited there directly; the channel set in `vz_chans` is reduced or extended to match.

The 7-day base (`average_days`) contains exactly one full week structure — every weekday appears once, the average is balanced across the week. `day_weights_pct` lets individual days be weighted more heavily (see Configuration), e.g. yesterday and the same weekday of the previous week; without weighting every day counts equally.

## CO₂-intensity profile

The zone split (red/green) comes from **SMARD** (Bundesnetzagentur) — real day-ahead grid data, free and without registration. SMARD is a prerequisite; there is no alternative source of real grid data. Beyond the one-day cache, an outage falls back to a modelled emergency mode (see When the SMARD fetch fails).

`dirt_shift` fetches the forecasted wind+solar generation and forecasted total consumption for today **and** tomorrow, and builds the hourly ratio of renewables to load from them.

**When the load forecast is missing, the profile of the most recent available day is used.** SMARD publishes the two series independently, and the load forecast routinely lags the generation forecast by a day or more. Since a day is only usable when **both** series cover it, that lag alone took the whole program out — even though the generation curve, the very quantity that shapes the daily dirtiness pattern, was there. Substituting is defensible because the median cut only reads the **shape** of the ratio across the window, never its absolute level; load profiles repeat closely enough from day to day for the shape to survive. On an unusual day (holiday, heatwave) the split shifts slightly — a far smaller error than having no zones at all. The search walks back a day at a time for up to seven days, so the substitute is always the freshest available; with `-v` the substitution is reported. If the **generation** forecast is missing instead, the day cannot be judged at all and is dropped. From both days it then assembles a **rolling 24-hour array**, anchored at the current time: hours from now until midnight come from today, hours after midnight from tomorrow — an hour that looks 'already past' in the array is really the next upcoming occurrence of that hour tomorrow. If tomorrow's day-ahead forecast for a given hour is not published yet (typically before late afternoon) or unavailable entirely, that hour uses today's figure.

Only over that assembled window is the **median** taken, **once**: hours with a ratio at or above it become **green**, those below become **red** — a cut that follows the actual spread rather than a fixed percentage, still separating the relatively cleaner hours from the worst ones even in a uniformly dirty window. Being a median, the cut always yields roughly 12 red and 12 green hours; degenerating to all-green or all-red is structurally impossible.

**Why over the window and not per calendar day:** cutting each day separately makes 'red' mean 'dirty *for that day*', which says nothing across the midnight boundary the window crosses every evening. If today's midday was exceptionally clean the day's median sits high, so a genuinely dirty late-evening hour still falls below it and counts green, while the next day's calmer hours — absolutely cleaner — count red. The battery was then stopped in the dirtier hour to save charge for the cleaner ones, the exact inverse of the intent. One cut over the 24 hours actually being decided about removes that, and puts the zones on the same footing as the wallbox's own median test (see there), which is computed over the same rolling window.

**When the SMARD fetch fails:** the cached data may substitute for exactly **one** more day — the cache carries a `fetch_date`, and data fetched yesterday still passes, because its 'tomorrow' half was the day-ahead forecast for what is now today. Anything older, or no cache at all, triggers an **emergency mode**: from the weather forecast (solar and wind via `wind_speed_100m` fetched in **one** Open-Meteo call and cached together, hourly like the radiation) and a fixed normalised profile of the **nationwide grid load**, `dirt_shift` models a *relative* dirtiness shape and cuts it at its median as usual. Deliberately coarse and never calibrated — only the ordering of hours feeds the cut — but it accounts for wind, so a windy night comes out cleaner than a dull midday (which a solar-only guess would get backwards). The emergency mode is **clearly flagged** in every output and never cached. If the weather forecast cannot be fetched either, `dirt_shift` aborts hard, leaving an all-allowed timer behind.

Two zones:

- **red** (dirtier half) — hours with a renewables ratio below the rolling window's median
- **green** (cleaner half) — hours with a ratio at or above that median

## PV reference curve

`dirt_shift` needs an estimate of how much PV yield to expect for the rest of the day. Instead of a physical model of the roof area(s) — which would be tedious to maintain with multiple sub-areas of different orientation and seasonally shifting shading — `dirt_shift` uses the installation's **own, actually measured** generation: for each hour of the day, the 95th percentile (`PV_CURVE_PERCENTILE`) of hourly PV values over the last 14 days (`PV_CURVE_DAYS`) is taken — close to the maximum, without one record day skewing the curve. Since the curve comes from the installation itself, it automatically reflects its actual geometry (multiple sub-areas, shading) without anything about tilt, orientation, or shading needing to be configured.

The curve is recomputed **once daily** starting at `PV_CURVE_REFRESH_HOUR` (4 a.m., a quiet time before sunrise with no competing same-day data) and cached in `dirt_pv_curve_cache.json` — independent of the hourly rhythm of the `basic_load` averaging. The underlying query uses the same hourly resolution (`group=hour`) as the `basic_load` query, not minute values.

## Radiation-forecast scaling

The PV reference curve shows what to expect on a **typical** day — it knows nothing about today's actual weather. That gap is filled by a free, registration-free radiation forecast from **Open-Meteo** (`shortwave_radiation`, W/m², hourly, for today). `shortwave_radiation` is the global horizontal irradiance (direct plus diffuse component together) — Open-Meteo's physical model output for the actual radiative power reaching the ground.

A **clear-sky index** is formed from the radiation forecast: `expected_pv = reference value × min(1, radiation forecast / clear-sky GHI)`. `clear-sky GHI` is the modelled global irradiance under a cloudless sky for the same hour and location, from the Haurwitz (1945) clear-sky model: `GHI = 1098 × cos(z) × exp(−0.059 / cos(z))` for zenith angle `z` (derived from solar elevation, see `solar_elevation_deg`/`clear_sky_ghi`), else 0 (sun below the horizon). The model needs only the solar position — no turbidity/aerosol data — so it is computable offline. The index is capped at 1.0 (brief cloud-edge radiation enhancement above the clear-sky value is not modelled, to keep the forecast conservative).

This automatically yields a season- and time-of-day-dependent reference: in winter, midday clear-sky GHI is substantially lower than in summer (shallower sun angle), so the same measured radiation value produces a higher clear-sky index (less damping) in winter than in summer at the same absolute irradiance — matching physical reality.

Each request fetches the full 48-hour series (today **and** tomorrow) in one call; Open-Meteo maintains a continuously updated timeseries in which each new model run is stitched seamlessly onto the previous one — even already-elapsed hours of today get overwritten with the latest available model state. From the two 24-value series (today/tomorrow), `dirt_shift` builds a **rolling 24-hour array**, anchored at the current time: hours from now until midnight come from today's series, hours after midnight come from tomorrow's — an hour that looks 'already past' in the array is really the genuine forecast for the next upcoming occurrence of that hour tomorrow. `clear_sky_ghi` is evaluated against tomorrow's date rather than today's for those hours accordingly. An hour missing from tomorrow's series falls back to today's value for that hour.

The full 48-hour series is refetched hourly and cached in `dirt_weather_cache.json`, independent of the other caches. If it is unavailable (fetch failed), the reference curve is used unscaled. If the reference curve itself is also unavailable (no PV forecast at all, e.g. a fresh install without 14 days of history), `dirt_shift` proceeds assuming a zero-PV day — conservative, but still functional (see Red reserve and the dirtiest hour).

## Discharge by zone

On each run (quarter-hourly), the current zone, the battery content relative to the red reserve, and — in red — whether the running hour is the dirtiest one in the current window determine discharge behaviour:

- **green, content > reserve** → **no limit**: the battery discharges freely.
- **green, content ≤ reserve, reserved red hours at least 10 `dirt%` points dirtier than now** → **no battery discharge**: `pvpt` only (`000 100 000`). An exact tie (content equal to the reserve) counts as "not yet reached" — stop, not free.
- **green, content ≤ reserve, gap below 10 points** → **capped** as in a non-priority red hour: the battery covers the house's share, nothing more.

**Why the minimum gap:** shifting 1 Wh from now into a later hour pays off in proportion to the `dirt%` difference between the two. If the reserved night is only a few points above the current hour, the gain sits inside forecast noise — while the grid import now is certain. The median cut alone does not know this magnitude: it makes the dirtier half red even when it is dirtier by a single point. The comparison is against the **mean** of the red hours the reserve is held for (those `red_window_demand` counts, or the next red block if `now` sits inside a surplus) — the reserve is spent across the whole block, not in a single hour. The gap is a **difference** in points, not a fraction: `dirt%` is signed and goes negative on windy days, where a fraction of the median would invert its own meaning. `STOP_MIN_DIRT_GAP` (10) is a named constant in the code, not a configuration option. If a `dirt%` value is missing on either side, the cautious side applies: stop. The `-v` mode line shows the gap as `[red block +N dirt]` or `[red block only +N dirt < 10: limit, not stop]`.
- **red, content ≥ reserve** → **no limit**: the reserve is comfortable, no throttling needed.
- **red, content < reserve, running hour is the dirtiest in the window** → **no limit**: this is where the (insufficient) reserve is deliberately spent.
- **red, content < reserve, running hour is *not* the dirtiest in the window** → **capped** to `limit_discharge_rate` (Watt, config) **and simultaneously** to a quarter-hour energy budget of `1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)` for that hour (see Red reserve and the dirtiest hour).

`pvpt` (direct PV pass-through) keeps running in every zone regardless; `dirt_shift` steers only the battery discharge.

**Why green is not simply free whenever the reserve has not yet been reached:** if a large load not captured in `basic_load` is deliberately served during the clean hours (typically an EV wallbox — deliberately excluded so it does not inflate the reserve calculation unnecessarily), a plain PV-vs-`basic_load` comparison would not see it: `basic_load` does not include the wallbox at all, so the hour would still look like a surplus hour on paper, even though the battery is really being drained by the wallbox. The categorical stop in green, as long as the reserve has not been reached, prevents that: ordinary house consumption keeps being covered by `pvpt`, while the wallbox spike on top of it is necessarily drawn from the grid, not the battery.

## Red reserve and the dirtiest hour

The **red reserve** is `reserve_pct` (default 90 %) of the `basic_load` demand across every **red** hour between now and the next **PV production phase**, **net** of any PV still expected during those hours. The window's boundary is the first hour whose expected PV exceeds its `basic_load` (a surplus hour): from there on the battery genuinely refills, and any later red span is covered by the coming yield, not by yesterday's charge — holding content back for it would only block storage room for the coming yield. Several separate red spans before that point (e.g. evening red and night red with a green gap between them) are all summed, since nothing refills the battery in between. On a day so dull that expected PV never exceeds load, no surplus hour exists — then all red hours of the rolling 24 h are reserved for, which is correct, as no refill is coming.

**A floor from a second estimate.** The window calculation above has a blind spot: while `now` itself sits inside an ongoing surplus stretch, the scan stops immediately and returns `0` — on the assumption that the coming yield will refill the battery anyway. That assumption collapses as soon as a load (a running wallbox above all) drains the battery faster than the surplus refills it: with `reserve = 0`, `content > reserve` is trivially true, the mode goes `free`, and the battery is handed over unlimited exactly when nothing is guarding it. `upcoming_red_demand` is therefore computed as well and the **larger** of the two applies (the wallbox uses the same formula, see there):

```
reserve = reserve_pct × max(red_window_demand,
                            max(0, upcoming_red_demand − surplus_before_next_red))
```

Neither estimate is redundant, because their blind spots are complementary: `red_window_demand` looks **broadly, but only up to the first surplus** (summing several separate red spans, and on a dull day the whole 24 hours), while `upcoming_red_demand` looks **narrowly, but across the surplus** (only the one next contiguous red block, yet visible even mid-surplus). Depending on the day, either can be the binding one.

**The expected surplus is subtracted from it.** The assumption `red_window_demand` originally made — discharge overnight and through the morning, let PV recharge from midday on, discharge again in the red evening — is simply right on a sunny day; it was just never checked. `surplus_before_next_red` therefore sums the expected surplus of the hours between now and the start of the next red block and subtracts it from the demand. Only what the surplus does **not** cover is held back:

- If it covers the gap several times over (a typical sunny day), the reserve drops to 0 — the battery may discharge through the morning exactly as intended, and refills itself in the afternoon.
- If it covers part of it, the uncovered remainder stays.
- If no surplus lies before the red block at all (evening, night, dull day), nothing changes — the protection applies in full.

The transition is therefore continuous, measuring how far the assumption holds instead of believing or discarding it wholesale.

This floor applies not only during a surplus stretch but in any window that precedes a surplus — including green hours at night, when the red block only comes after tomorrow's yield. The battery no longer discharges there while its content fails to cover the next red block: during a clean hour the house is meant to be served from the grid, with the content kept for the dirty hours — precisely the point of the program.

On the netting: `pvpt` already covers part of that demand directly (at the edges of a red span, while the sun is not quite gone or already back), so that share need not additionally be reserved from the battery. The 90 % deliberately keep the reserve from exceeding the computed demand — under normal circumstances the battery is meant to discharge essentially fully over the red window, rather than holding capacity back unused.

### When the reserve runs short: the dirtiest hour first

If current content does not cover the reserve (`content < reserve`), every red hour is no longer served unrestricted by default. Instead, within the same window (now up to the next surplus hour), the **one** red hour with the highest `dirt%` is identified — ties go to the chronologically earliest one in the window. Only that hour stays unrestricted; every other red hour in the window is capped simultaneously to `limit_discharge_rate` (Watt) **and** to `1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)` for that hour (Wh, quarter-hour energy budget) (see Discharge by zone).

The window is rebuilt fresh from the current time on **every** run — an already-past dirtiest hour simply falls out of the (now shorter) window on the next run, and the dirtiest hour remaining then automatically becomes free, without any special-case rule needed for that. Green hours within the window do not count either way — only red hours are compared against each other.

The `-v` output shows the identified dirtiest hour in the line `content ... Wh (SoC%)   reserve(...%) ... Wh [window|upcoming: .../...-...pv] -> dirtiest  HH:MM   =>  mode: ...` (the value after `content` is the state of charge in percent of `battery_capacity_wh`); the bracket names which of the two estimates was binding, followed by both raw values (`red_window_demand`/`upcoming_red_demand`) and the surplus subtracted from the second (`...pv`), all before the `reserve_pct` scaling; in the `-debug` table, that same hour carries a leading `!` on its `chg` tag (`!D`, versus plain `D` for every other discharging hour). The `chg` column also shows `L` for every charging hour (`exp_PV > basic_load`), with the same `!` marker (`!L`) for the cleanest charging hour of the whole day — purely informational, no effect on the decision. A separate `balance` column shows `exp_PV − basic_load`, signed (blank when `exp_PV = 0`, since it would then just restate `basic_load`). Hours that the rolling array is meant to represent as "tomorrow" but that still fall back to today's values for lack of a real tomorrow-specific value yet (see CO₂-intensity profile and Radiation-forecast scaling) carry a leading `.` — independently on `rad_Wm2` (radiation forecast) and `dirt%` (SMARD classification), depending on which of the two sources still lacks real tomorrow data for that hour. A final line sums `exp_PV` and `basic_load` each across all 24 hours, their difference (the day's net balance), and the unweighted `dirt%` average (`Ø`) across every SMARD-covered hour.

The priority acts at exactly one point: which hour gets `free` instead of `limit`. It does not reserve any Wh explicitly for the dirtiest hour against the other red hours in the same window — instead, a non-priority red hour is capped, via the quarter-hour energy budget itself, to `min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV` per hour (four quarter-hours of `1/4` of that each). `reserve_pct` scales the same share of demand here as in the reserve calculation itself; the measured PV is subtracted in full regardless, since it flows unrestricted via `pvpt` anyway and so needs no proportional "reserving". The additional `limit_discharge_rate` cap (Watt) only limits instantaneous power within the quarter-hour, not the total energy drawn.

## Precharge (optional, off by default)

With `precharge_enabled: true`, `dirt_shift` can divert PV surplus into the battery that would otherwise have gone to the house via `pvpt` (see `precharge_ac_pct`) — the one exception to the `pvpt` guarantee (see Purpose). Precharge is the **mirror image of the discharge logic**: just as discharge is shifted into dirty hours, charging is pulled into clean ones. It spans the **very cleanest** hours of the window, no longer only the single cleanest one.

A green hour is throttled only if all three of the following hold — the same yardsticks used elsewhere in the controller:

**1. Rank.** The hour must be among the cleanest `precharge_cleanest_pct` % of the rolling window (see `_dirt_rank_pct`, as for the wallbox). This is what widens precharge from the single cleanest hour to all the very clean ones: several hours throttle together, each contributing part of the target.

**2. Gap.** The hour's `dirt%` must be at least `STOP_MIN_DIRT_GAP` (code constant, 10) points **below** the mean `dirt%` of the red hours being charged for (see `reserved_red_hours`). Shifting 1 Wh from here into them pays only in proportion to that difference; a gap inside forecast noise does not beat the round-trip loss. The same difference-not-fraction measure `stop` uses, sign-safe.

**3. Overflow.** Would anything actually be lost without diverting? The **total** expected charging surplus of the clean hours up to the red block — **the current hour included** — must exceed the battery's **free room** (`battery_capacity_wh − content`). Only the part that no longer fits is otherwise lost, and only that overflow is worth diverting. `pvpt` itself is lossless, the battery detour is not — diverting surplus that would have fit anyway just wastes the round-trip. Counting the current hour matters: were only the *other* hours counted, each large surplus hour would exclude itself from its own overflow test and believe it was indispensable — the exact bug that throttled the sunniest hours on a day where the battery fills itself several times over.

**Throttle depth.** The overflow is diverted, spread across the qualifying hours in proportion to each one's surplus; `pvpt` drops only by the diverted watts:

```
overflow = Σ(surplus of the clean hours, now included) − free room
divert   = overflow × (surplus[now] / Σ surplus)
ac_% = max(PRECHARGE_AC_FLOOR, round(100 × (exp_PV[now] − divert) / exp_PV[now]))
```

`PRECHARGE_AC_FLOOR` (code constant, 20) is a hard floor: a precharge decision must never drop the inverter feed so far that zeroinput rejects the line as no valid feed (`timer.txt enabled but not active`). Self-limiting and memoryless like the rest: as `content` rises the free room shrinks, the overflow with it, and `ac_%` climbs back to 100. If nothing overflows (free room larger than all the coming surplus), precharge does not engage at all — on a small, habitually empty battery that is the normal case.

The failsafe (see there) applies here too: a plain `ac_%` cap without a simultaneous discharge limit also triggers the 30-minute all-allowed line.

## Wallbox (optional, off by default)

With `wallbox_enabled: true`, `dirt_shift` switches an EV wallbox's relay via a Tasmota device — a mechanism independent of the existing `basic_load` exclusion (see Discharge by zone) and additional to it.

**No presence or charging-state detection.** Without feedback on the vehicle's state of charge (no SoC), no meaningful daily target can be formed — `dirt_shift` does not even attempt one. The relay is simply held closed whenever the conditions are met; if no vehicle is connected, nothing happens at all (no charging current, no harm).

**Switching on and switching off are not opposites of each other** — the criteria depend on whether `dirt_shift` currently owns the relay (see Owner marker below):

### One single criterion, checked continuously

Unlike one might expect, there are **no** separate criteria for switching on versus switching off — the same formula applies continuously, freshly on every run, regardless of whether `dirt_shift` already owns the relay:

```
should_on = dirtiness condition   OR   energy condition   OR   voltage condition
```

**Dirtiness condition** — two limits, both must hold:
```
rank of dirt%[now] within the window ≤ wallbox_cleanest_pct %   AND
dirt%[now] ≤ wallbox_absolute_max
```
`wallbox_cleanest_pct` (default `30`, in percent) requires the current hour to rank among the cleanest that-many percent of the rolling window — its own, tighter standard than the general red/green cut, taken over the same window.

**A rank, not a share of the median.** `dirt%` is a signed quantity: it goes negative whenever renewable generation exceeds load, which on a windy, sunny day happens for many hours at a stretch. A percentage *of* it carries no stable meaning: the same setting would let nothing through on a uniformly dirty day and most of the window through on a windy one. A rank is defined on the ordering alone and is therefore immune to sign, offset and scale; the setting admits exactly the stated share of hours whatever the data looks like. Ties count as cleaner, so identical hours are treated alike. `wallbox_absolute_max` (default `50`) keeps the relative threshold alone from still letting a meaningfully dirty hour through on a uniformly dirty day (high median). The median is computed across all 24 hours of the rolling day (see CO₂-intensity profile), not just the remaining ones.

**The discharge mode plays no part in this condition.** It answers exactly one question — is now a good time to charge the car from the *grid*? — and whether the battery could lose out by it is a different question, answered elsewhere (see "Battery-side protection" below). Folding both into one condition would wire the protection backwards: `free` is the one mode in which the battery may be discharged without limit, and so the one in which a wallbox could drain it — `stop`, by contrast, blocks all discharge. Gating on the mode would therefore permit the wallbox precisely where the battery is exposed and forbid it where it cannot be touched at all.

**Energy condition** — independent of dirtiness:
```
content − headroom  >  reserve_pct × max(red_window_demand, upcoming_red_demand)
margin   = wallbox_typical_power × 0.25 h   (one quarter hour of draw)
headroom = 2 × margin to switch on  |  1 × margin while this path is itself holding
```
The demand estimate is the red reserve's (see there), the larger of `red_window_demand` and `upcoming_red_demand` — **without** the expected-surplus subtraction applied there. That is deliberate and makes the wallbox stricter than the battery: a running wallbox is precisely the consumer that would eat that surplus before it ever reaches the battery, so it must not be assumed available for this check. The asymmetry only ever runs this way round — the wallbox stricter, never laxer. Both are needed, and neither may stand alone. `red_window_demand` reads `0` while `now` sits inside an assumed surplus stretch — on that alone the check would be a rubber stamp exactly when it matters most, since a running wallbox can draw more than the actual remaining surplus provides. `upcoming_red_demand` never reads `0` there, but stops at the first green hour: a short green gap before dawn truncates it to a fraction of what is really needed before PV returns — and on that alone a wallbox will happily empty the battery overnight against a reserve that never saw those hours. **The wallbox must not be held to a lower bar than the battery it draws from.**

The reserve is deliberately compared **directly against current `content`**, with no projected future surplus added on top — that assumed surplus is exactly what a running wallbox could itself consume before it ever reaches the battery.

**Why two thresholds (the energy condition's hysteresis):** `margin` is exactly **one** run's worth of draw. Switching on with less than that to spare is undone by its own consequence at the very next run — the wallbox flaps on and off at the quarter hour without ever charging anything worth the name. The factor `2` at switch-on therefore demands at least one run of buffer before the check can flip. It is expressed as a factor on `margin` rather than a fixed Wh value so it scales with the actual wallbox power: the flapping period is the run interval, so the measure that matters is run-lengths of charging, not an absolute amount of energy. Both thresholds are named constants in the code, not a configuration option.

As with the voltage condition, this hysteresis state is tracked **separately from the owner marker** (key `energy_on`, see below) — the lower holding threshold must not prop up a relay some other path switched on.

**Voltage condition** — the full battery:
```
min(Vbat over the last 15 min)  ≥  threshold × cell_count
threshold = 3.375 V/cell to switch on  |  3.25 V/cell while this path is itself holding
```
If battery voltage has **not** dropped below the threshold over the past quarter hour, the battery counts as full and still being fed by PV — surplus that would otherwise go to waste can go into the car instead. Testing the **minimum** over a window rather than the instantaneous value checks two things at once: high state of charge *and* the absence of any meaningful discharge load during that time. A single sample could happen to be read in the middle of a brief load spike.

Both thresholds are defined **per cell** and scaled by `cell_count`, exactly like the empty anchor of the energy-content calculation (see Data sources) — so 54.0 V and 52.0 V respectively at 16S. They are named constants in the code, not a configuration option.

**Why two thresholds (the voltage condition's hysteresis):** voltage also stays high precisely *because* the wallbox is off. Once it switches on it draws load, voltage sags — and without the lower holding threshold the very condition that just switched it on would fall away: a flapping at the quarter hour. Once engaged, the path therefore holds down to 3.25 V/cell; only after that does it need the full recovery to 3.375 V/cell again.

This hysteresis state is tracked **separately from the owner marker** (key `voltage_on` in the same file, see below). Otherwise the lower holding threshold would also apply to a relay the dirtiness condition switched on during a clean grid — it would then stay on via voltage long after the grid turned dirty.

**The voltage condition does not simply restate the energy condition.** Precisely when a long dirty stretch lies ahead, the demand estimate and with it the wallbox-specific reserve is large — the energy condition then stays unmet even though the battery is physically full and cannot absorb another Wh. That is the gap the voltage measurement closes. It is also a **direct measurement**, so it inherits none of the drift in the `content` estimate integrated since the last empty anchor. If the voltage cannot be fetched, this path simply counts as unmet — the run itself continues normally.

**Why all conditions apply continuously, not just at switch-on:** because the paths are meant to run side by side, on equal footing — an hour clean enough may charge even if the energy condition doesn't (yet) hold; a comfortably full battery may charge even during a dirty hour; and a full battery may hand over its otherwise wasted surplus even when both other conditions argue against it. A relay `dirt_shift` itself switched on therefore stays on as long as **at least one** of the three conditions still holds, and only switches off once **all** fail at the same time.

**On the voltage condition and `limit`/`stop`:** because the energy condition now uses the same reserve as the main mode, it can no longer keep a relay running while the battery itself is already being protected. That does not hold for the voltage condition, but there it is deliberate: a full battery should be allowed to hand over its surplus even under `limit`/`stop` — that is precisely the case it exists for (see below). Its own holding threshold bounds this: once voltage sags below 3.25 V/cell, the path falls away.

One boundary condition follows from the dirtiness condition: in the red zone, `free` also holds at the **dirtiest hour itself** (where the reserve is meant to be deliberately spent), regardless of battery content. On a uniformly "deep red" night where no hour clears both dirtiness thresholds on its own, the wallbox may therefore still start charging during that one reddest hour, provided it itself stays under `wallbox_absolute_max` — a deliberately accepted boundary condition, not a gap.

**Precharge (see there) has no bearing on the wallbox** — the two mechanisms run fully independently of each other.

### Battery-side protection

The wallbox decision is made **before** the timer line is written, and its outcome feeds into the discharge mode:

```
wallbox running  AND  mode would be free  AND  content ≤ reserve  AND  neither energy nor voltage condition  →  mode becomes limit
```

`free` hands the battery over without limit — the one mode in which a running wallbox can drain it. But it is downgraded only when the car would actually pull the battery **below the reserve**, i.e. when `content ≤ reserve`: if content already covers the reserve, the surplus above it may go to the car and the battery still ends the slot above its reserve. In green, `free` already means `content > reserve`, so the downgrade only ever bites in the one red hour where `free` is forced regardless of content (the dirtiest hour) — there the forced discharge must not be spent on the car. Where it applies, discharge is capped to the house's share for this slot (rate cap and slot budget as under `limit`, see Output: timer.txt) and the wallbox's draw lands on the grid. Without the reserve condition, the downgrade stopped discharge entirely on a sunny day with the reserve at 0, even though the battery refills itself anyway and nothing needed protecting. `stop` and `limit` already protect the battery and are left unchanged.

The three conditions thus each name the energy source the car charges from: dirtiness → grid, energy → battery surplus, voltage → PV surplus. And the battery's protection sits where battery discharge is actually controlled, instead of as a side condition inside the wallbox logic.

The `-v` output marks the downgrade in the mode line with `[free->limit: wallbox on grid terms]`.

### Owner marker

`dirt_shift` may only switch the relay off if it is itself the reason it is currently on — a manual activation is never undone, however dirty the grid gets. A small persistent file (`dirt_wallbox_marker.json`) tracks whether `dirt_shift` currently has the relay switched on:

- **Switching on** is always unproblematic (a no-op if already on) — the marker is set to `true`.
- **Switching off** only happens if the marker is `true`.
- **The very first run with no history**: if the relay is already on at that point, it is treated as foreign by default (marker `false`) — `dirt_shift` leaves it alone.

**Consequence for manual intervention:** if someone switches the relay on manually while it is currently dirty, it stays untouched — across multiple runs, if needed. But as soon as the **next** hour arrives that meets the conditions, `dirt_shift` silently "adopts" the relay (sets the marker to `true`, without actually changing anything — it was already running). From that point on it counts as dirt_shift's own again and can be switched off normally at the next dirty hour. The manual protection therefore only covers the currently ongoing dirty stretch, not permanently.

This detection has a known limit: if someone switches manually **while** the marker is already `true`, `dirt_shift` cannot distinguish that from its own prior action — Tasmota's status shows only the state, not who caused it. That remains an accepted limitation, not a gap still to be closed.

The same file carries **two further, independent keys**: `energy_on` and `voltage_on`, the hysteresis states of the energy and voltage conditions respectively (see above). Each says only whether that one path is currently holding, and both are rewritten on **every** run — including runs where the relay itself does not change. All three keys are written independently, without overwriting one another. If either hysteresis key is absent (e.g. in a marker file from an earlier run), it counts as `false`, so the path in question starts on its stricter switch-on threshold.

### Switching with verification

Switching uses a direct Tasmota `Power{output}` command (HTTP GET), **not** a Tasmota timer — unlike `tib_zero_tas`, whose price-driven schedule is known in advance. Here, every run needs to be able to react (a zone change, a mode change), which only fits a direct command issued during the run itself.

The command alone is not enough: Tasmota accepts an HTTP command even if the device becomes unreachable afterwards, or the switch does not actually take effect. So after every set command, the actual relay status is re-queried (`Power{output}` with no value) — up to **3 attempts**, **30 seconds** apart. The owner marker is only updated after a **verified** state change; if all 3 attempts fail, the marker is left unchanged.

**Runs at the very end of `main()`**, after `write_timer` and the dirtiness export — deliberately placed there so that a hung or unreachable Tasmota device (up to ~90 seconds in the worst case, from the retry delays) never delays writing `timer.txt`. Neither success nor failure affects `dirt_shift`'s return value — both are only reported under `-v`, exactly like the dirtiness-value export.

## Failsafe

`dirt_shift` is optional and must never block zeroinput's normal operation. If a run limits or stops discharge, or caps `ac_%` below 100 (see Precharge), it additionally writes an "all-allowed" line (`100 100 -1`) 30 minutes later. As long as the script keeps running, the restriction is renewed every 15 minutes; if it stops running (cron failure, volkszähler unreachable, crash), the restriction lifts itself after 30 minutes, and zeroinput discharges freely again, as if `dirt_shift` did not exist. In free mode without a precharge cap, everything is already allowed, so the one line suffices there.

## Exporting grid dirtiness

`dirt_shift` can additionally log the current dirtiness value to volkszähler — on every run, if `vz_dirtiness_uuid` is set. The value is `(1 − ratio) × 100` (renewables/load for the current hour): the sign convention matches the installation's existing power channels (Import positive, Inverter negative) — the more positive, the dirtier (below-average renewable share); on a renewable surplus (ratio > 1) the value even goes negative, like a feed-in.

The write is a direct **HTTP POST** to volkszähler's middleware API, once per run: `http://{vz_host_port}/data/{vz_dirtiness_uuid}.json`, with the value and current timestamp. It reuses the same `vz_host_port` dirt_shift already uses for its other volkszähler queries — no vzlogger meter, no local file. `vz_dirtiness_uuid` must be a real channel UUID, created in volkszähler beforehand. A failed write (network error, wrong UUID) does not abort the run, it is only reported under `-v`.

## Output: timer.txt

`dirt_shift` writes `timer.txt` in the zeroinput format:

```
YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:00  <discharge-W>  <ac-%>  <energy-Wh>
```

- Each line carries the real calendar date it was written for.
- **discharge-W** — discharge cap; `100` (percent) = no limit, `limit_discharge_rate` (Watt, config) = capped, `000` = no battery discharge (stop).
- **ac-%** — inverter pass-through, `100` (pvpt guaranteed), except during an active precharge cap (see there): then a continuous value between `0` and `100` for each qualifying very clean green hour.
- **energy-Wh** — energy budget; in capped mode `1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)` for that hour, floored at 0 (the actual Wh allotment for exactly the running quarter-hour), otherwise `-1` = genuinely unlimited (a sentinel `zeroinput.py`'s `discharge_times.update()` recognises and skips the budget check for entirely), `000` = no budget (stop).

The three modes are thus: `100 100 -1` (no limit), `<limit_discharge_rate> 100 <1/4×(min(100 %,reserve_pct)×basic_load−PV_now)>` (capped), `000 100 000` (stop). Capped mode applies two caps at once, each closing the gap the other leaves open: `limit_discharge_rate` limits only instantaneous power — a load that stays permanently below this threshold but never lets up would otherwise run past it unrestricted. The Wh field instead limits the total energy drawn in that quarter-hour to `1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)` — `reserve_pct` (default 90%) scales the same share of demand the reserve calculation itself assumes, rather than letting a non-priority hour draw its full demand; PV is subtracted in full regardless, since `pvpt` already passes it through directly and it need not also come from the battery. Drawing an hour's worth long enough (Watt) and high enough (Wh) at once therefore evades neither cap. `limit_discharge_rate` is an installation-specific setting; the Wh budget is fixed and needs no configuration entry of its own, since it follows directly from `basic_load`, `reserve_pct`, and the measured PV.

**This uses measured PV, not the forecast.** The budget governs the quarter hour happening *right now*, and an hourly mean is too coarse for that: on the ramp hours at each end of the day — which are also, routinely, the marginal red hours — the mean can still sit near `basic_load` while real output has long collapsed. The budget would then fall to a few Wh in exactly the quarter hour where the battery should be carrying the house, and the shortfall would come from the grid during a red hour. `dirt_shift` therefore queries the `PV` channel for the last 15 minutes and uses that actual figure; the forecast remains the fallback when the measurement is unavailable. Everything about the future (reserve, dirtiest hour, precharge) still relies on the forecast alone.

**`reserve_pct` is capped at 100% here — and only here.** For the reserve itself a value above 100 is meaningful and makes the system more cautious: it holds back more than the computed demand. For a cap the same value would invert that intent — above 100% the budget would exceed the hour's actual net demand, so the cap would stop restricting anything precisely when it is needed. Capping keeps a higher `reserve_pct` consistently more conservative on both sides.

Setting `limit_discharge_rate` very high effectively disables the power cap, leaving only the energy budget in effect.

Because `dirt_shift` rewrites the file every quarter hour, the timer file's last line changes on every run — a single quarter-hour's budget per run is therefore enough: `zeroinput.py`'s energy counter resets exactly then (see `discharge_times.update()`), so the next quarter-hour gets its own fresh budget. Only if `dirt_shift` itself stops running does the last written line persist and the counter stop resetting — the failsafe (see there) then takes over, switching to "all allowed" after 30 minutes regardless.

Values > 100 are interpreted as watts, values ≤ 100 as percent — as in the existing zeroinput timer format. zeroinput's `discharge_times` parser reads the lines in order and applies each line's values as long as its timestamp is in the past, stopping at the first line whose timestamp is still in the future (that is where it breaks) — the active state is always that of the last already-past line. Once dirt_shift stops running and both lines eventually fall into the past, the loop no longer breaks and runs through to the end instead, so the state settles on the **last** line in the file. Since that last line is always the failsafe line (`FREE`), or the single, already-free line in 'free' mode, the state settles into 'all allowed' on its own — without the file needing to be rewritten again.

The written plan is short: the current quarter-hour slot in the chosen mode (free / capped / stop), and — if the mode limits or stops — an "all-allowed" line 30 minutes later as a failsafe.

`dirt_shift` runs every quarter hour (e.g. via cron) and rewrites the file each time with the current energy content and zone.

## Configuration

`dirt_shift.conf` holds **only** the path to `zeroinput.conf` and dirt_shift's own parameters. Values already present in `zeroinput.conf` are read from there instead of being duplicated — `dirt_shift` never modifies `zeroinput.conf`.

Read from `zeroinput.conf` (read-only):

- **`discharge_t_file`** — the path of the timer file that zeroinput reads. `dirt_shift` writes exactly this file (resolved relative to zeroinput.conf). This guarantees writer and reader point at the same file. `discharge_timer` must additionally be enabled in `zeroinput.conf`, otherwise zeroinput ignores the file.

dirt_shift's own keys in `dirt_shift.conf`:

- `zeroinput_conf` — path to `zeroinput.conf` (default `../zeroinput.conf`, since dirt_shift usually lives in a subfolder of zeroinput)
- `vz_host_port`, `vz_chans` — volkszähler host and channel UUIDs for the data.json API. Separate from zeroinput's own `vz_channels`/`vzlogger_log_file`: dirt_shift uses the HTTP API for averages, PV curve, and energy content, zeroinput uses the vzlogger FIFO for live control. Both talk to the same volkszähler; the UUID lists need not match.
- `vz_dirtiness_uuid` — real volkszähler channel UUID for the dirtiness-value export via HTTP POST (see Exporting grid dirtiness). Empty disables the export.
- `average_days` — days for the hourly average (default 7)
- `day_weights_pct` — day weighting in percent for the average, chronological: index 0 = oldest day (today minus `average_days`, i.e. the same weekday of the previous week), index −1 = yesterday. Weighting yesterday and the same weekday of last week more heavily captures the most recent trend and the weekday structure. Length must match `average_days`; on mismatch every day is weighted equally. All 100 = neutral.
- `reserve_pct` — percent of the `basic_load` demand across the red window that is reserved (default 90); also scales the quarter-hour energy budget of non-priority red hours (see Output: timer.txt, where it is capped at 100%) and the wallbox-specific reserve estimate (see Wallbox). Values above 100 are allowed and make the reserve itself more cautious
- `limit_discharge_rate` — Watt cap on discharge power during a non-priority red hour (default 3000); installation-specific, adapt to your own ordinary peak load. Applies together with the fixed quarter-hour energy budget (`1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)`, floored at 0, not a config value of its own). Set very high, the power cap is effectively no longer in effect, leaving only the energy budget.
- `latitude`, `longitude` — the installation's location (decimal degrees) for the clear-sky model and the radiation forecast; default ~centre of Germany (51.0 / 10.0)
- `PV_to_bat_efficiency`, `bat_to_AC_efficiency` — efficiencies for reconstructing the energy content
- `max_days_empty_battery` — how many days back to search for an 'empty' state
- `disable_zeroinput_timer` — set to `true` to compute and print without writing the timer file (dry run)
- `precharge_enabled` — optional, default `false`. Enables the precharge path (see there), which may cap `ac_%` below 100 in the very cleanest green hours to deliberately divert PV surplus into the battery instead of the house.
- `precharge_cleanest_pct` — rank threshold for precharge, in percent (default 25): only green hours among the cleanest that-many percent of the window are throttled.
- `battery_capacity_wh` — usable battery capacity in Wh (default 5000). For precharge: surplus is diverted only when the expected surplus exceeds the free room (`battery_capacity_wh − content`), i.e. when it would otherwise be lost. Also drives the state-of-charge display (`content ... Wh (SoC%)`) in the `-v` output.
- `wallbox_enabled` — optional, default `false`. Enables wallbox control (see there).
- `wallbox_ip` — IP address of the Tasmota device switching the wallbox relay.
- `wallbox_output` — Tasmota relay/output number (default `"1"`).
- `wallbox_cleanest_pct` — the wallbox may only run while the current hour ranks among the cleanest this-many percent of the rolling window (default `30`).
- `wallbox_absolute_max` — absolute dirtiness threshold for the wallbox, in `dirt%` (default `50`).
- `wallbox_typical_power` — typical wallbox charging power in watts (default `2000`), used only for the quarter-hour look-ahead of the energy condition (see Wallbox) — not a metered value, a safety margin.

The quarter-hour energy budget (`1/4 × (min(100 %, reserve_pct) × basic_load − measured PV)`, see Output: timer.txt) is **not** a configuration option of its own — it follows directly from `basic_load`, `reserve_pct`, and the measured PV power and therefore needs no value of its own in `dirt_shift.conf`.

### Error behaviour

Missing data does not abort where it need not. If the battery energy content is missing, the battery is freed (the reserve logic depends on it) while the wallbox is still steered via its redundant paths; if all wallbox paths are undecidable for lack of data, the relay is left unchanged. If SMARD is missing, the modelled emergency zones drive the whole run. Only on a genuinely hard error (no complete days for the average, or neither SMARD nor the weather model available) does `dirt_shift` abort, but first — if the timer path is known — writes an "all-allowed" line so zeroinput is not blocked by a stale or missing limit; the wallbox relay is left untouched:

```
2026-07-09 00:00:00 100 100 -1
```

(full discharge, full pass-through, effectively unlimited energy budget, dated with the current date). If not even `zeroinput.conf` is readable (timer path unknown), only the abort with an error message remains.
